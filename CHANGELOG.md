- **seekthumbs reads 1.4 MB per thumbnail instead of 5.7 MB on a 1080p H.264 remux, and 1.6 MB on a 4K HEVC Dolby Vision remux.** A seek lands on the keyframe before the target, and the decoder (frame threads, B-frame reordering) does not give that keyframe back until it has been fed more packets, which the old loop read from the source and then threw away. The new default `keyframe_flush` decodes the keyframe's own packet and drains the decoder. That remux, 60 thumbnails, 4 reads at a time: 1.4 MB average, 3.1 MB worst, 1.0 packet per grab, all 60 by the flush, read phase 91 s against 199 s; with `keyframe_flush_check 60` every flushed read was repeated the old way and the frame timestamps matched 60 of 60 (timestamps, not pixels). The 4K remux (65.5 GB, 23 thumbnails, flush on): 1.6 MB average, 2.1 MB worst, 10 of 10 checked frames matched. Owner spot-check: the thumbnails of one 2160p Dolby Vision HYBRID HEVC remux built with the flush were all viewed and looked correct (a visual check of one film, not a pixel comparison). The same 4K film with the flush off: 10.5 MB average and 16.8 MB worst per thumbnail, 23.5 packets per grab, read phase 140 s against 79 s, so about 6.5 times fewer bytes with the flush (one run each, flush on first). **Not done:** other codecs, a wider batch of films, an older PyAV, Dolby Vision profile 5.
- **seekthumbs, first batch on the owner's library:** 14 films (2160p HEVC with DV/HDR10/HDR10+ and 1080p AVC remuxes), 5,566 thumbnails, all by the flush, 0 failed reads, 1.7 MB per thumbnail on average (worst 5.8 MB), 11.0 GB received in total, 88 to 315 s per film; 13 other releases aliased in seconds; 4 releases of 3 films failed the HTTP probe with a 5XX within 1 to 2 s (cause not yet known); 16 parallel reads gave only about 17 % more reads per second than 10 on the 4K films (2.9 to 3.4), so a ceiling other than the worker count is in play (details in the seekthumbs README).
- **seekthumbs no longer falls back to the mount when a WebDAV read fails.** On a 43.8 GB film the `dav` probe returned a 5XX, the build continued through the mount without saying why, and received 47.6 GB, 46.0 MB per thumbnail. A failed HTTP probe now writes the full error to `STORE/.http-errors.jsonl`, records the film as failed and leaves it for the retry back-off. Why that one probe failed is not established.
- **What a Real-Debrid request costs (measured, a handful of requests, fixed order, so rough):** the `download` host closes every connection, so each data read pays a TCP and TLS handshake (about 0.4 of roughly 0.5 to 0.7 s to the first byte); `dav` keeps connections alive but redirects each request to a new connection on that host. A persistent connection would therefore not help, and was not built.
- **Correction: the "3 to 4 minutes per 4K film" figure for the 2026-10-28 live queue was wrong.** It came from log lines of the films that were checked. The owner reports that no film in that queue finished in under 12 to 15 minutes, and the average was over 25. The 2026-11-08 batch (10 parallel reads, keyframe flush) built seven 4K and 1080p remuxes in 100 to 160 s each and a 903-thumbnail one in 315 s, so the improvement over that queue is several-fold (roughly 5 to 10 times at best, more against the average), not the 2 times the wrong baseline implied; the conditions differ (more reads at once, the flush, other changes since), so it is not a controlled comparison.
- **Correction to the 2026-10-28 entries below:** the `bytes_limit_reached` refusal came from Decypharr, not Real-Debrid. The owner's own checks: Stremio, DebridMediaManager and Real-Debrid's website kept serving while Decypharr was blocked, and the account has unlimited bytes (the only limits known are 250 calls a minute and an unspecified fair-use policy).
